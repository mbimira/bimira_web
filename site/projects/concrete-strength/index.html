Predicting concrete compressive strength

Problem

Concrete strength is commonly measured by crushing test specimens after a specified curing period, often 28 days. This analysis asks whether mix ingredients and curing age can estimate measured strength from historical lab data. Such estimates may help screen candidate mixes, but they do not replace physical testing or established design standards.

Data

Public UCI Concrete Compressive Strength dataset — 1,030 lab records with seven mix ingredients and curing age as inputs, and compressive strength in MPa as the target. The analysis removes 25 exact duplicate rows, leaving 1,005 records for modeling.

Figure 1: Feature distributions and strength target across the 1,005 deduplicated records
Figure 2: Correlation matrix: which ingredients move with strength

Method

After removing exact duplicates, I split the 1,005 records by row into 804 training samples and 201 held-out samples. I tuned a neural network with cross-validation on the training data, then compared it with linear regression and gradient boosting. A separate nested evaluation uses five outer recipe-grouped folds and four inner grouped folds to tune the network without recipe overlap.

Figure 3: Permutation importance for the neural network; correlated inputs share importance

Permutation importance ranks log of curing age highest for this fitted neural network. Because inputs such as binder and cement overlap, the scores describe model reliance, not causal effects.

On the 201-row random holdout, the tuned neural network reaches R² 0.930, RMSE 4.565 MPa and MAE 2.917 MPa, about 38% lower RMSE than linear regression (7.402 MPa). Gradient boosting has slightly lower RMSE (4.454 MPa), while the neural network has slightly lower MAE. In nested recipe-grouped cross-validation, mean RMSE is 5.417 MPa for the neural network and 4.986 MPa for gradient boosting. These are internal cross-validation estimates, not external validation on new laboratory data.

Limitations

The nested recipe-grouped results estimate mean RMSE of 5.4 MPa for the neural network and 5.0 MPa for gradient boosting on this dataset. External data is still needed to confirm transfer to new materials and laboratories. The dataset covers lab-cured samples at controlled ages; field conditions (temperature, curing variability, aggregate moisture) are not represented. Validate predictions with physical testing before using them in engineering decisions.
